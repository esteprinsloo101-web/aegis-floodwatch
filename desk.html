Methods (screening equations)

River conveyance (Manning)

Q = (1/n) · A · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2)

Q discharge, n Manning roughness, A cross-section area, R hydraulic radius, S friction slope. Normal depth y is solved so computed Q matches a discharge estimate (e.g. GloFAS / Open-Meteo flood). Water-surface elevation: WSE = zmin + y. A DEM cell is treated as wet if terrain z < WSE.

GLOF / dam-break volume–time screen

Q_peak ≈ V_released / t_breach

Volume over breach duration — a weir-style screen, not a multi-day monsoon hydrograph.

Aftershocks (Omori–Utsu)

n(t) = K / (c + t)^p

Rate decay after a mainshock. Useful for planning windows; does not predict the next large event.

Tsunami deep-ocean speed

c ≈ √(g · H)

Long-wave speed in depth H. Travel-time screening only — coastal run-up needs dedicated models and official PTWC/NTWC products.

Wildfire screening (FWI family)

Fire Weather Index / Fosberg-style alignment of wind, temperature, humidity, and fuel dryness. Screens ignition / spread conditions — it does not timestamp a spark.

How to use with the free map

  1. Pin — on the free map, click an event and fly to its lat/lon.
  2. Source — read the popup source (EONET, USGS, GDACS if available). Prefer the named agency feed.
  3. Method — pick the matching equation above (Manning/WSE for river flood, √(gH) for tsunami travel screen, Omori–Utsu for aftershock windows, FWI for fire weather).
  4. Decision limits — treat AEGIS as screening. If SAWS or another official body issues a warning, that overrides this desk. Do not paywall escape guidance — Get me to safety stays free on the map.

Practical workflow (scientist / planner)

  1. Open the free live map; confirm layers (EONET, USGS, GDACS) and your region of interest (World / SA focus / GPS).
  2. List open hazards that intersect your basin, coast, or municipality.
  3. For floods: pull discharge or advisory context from GloFAS / Open-Meteo Flood or national hydro; apply Manning → WSE → wet-cell screen on a DEM you control.
  4. For GLOF / breach: estimate released volume and breach duration; compare Q_peak to channel capacity — escalate if valley floor is occupied.
  5. For seismicity: use USGS for what already happened; plan aftershock logistics with Omori–Utsu; never claim origin prediction.
  6. For tsunami watch: use √(gH) only for rough travel; defer to PTWC/NTWC and coastal authorities for evacuation.
  7. For fire weather: compute/consult FWI-class indices; stage resources on high-FWI days without inventing ignition times.
  8. Document assumptions (n, DEM date, ensemble member). Hand official warning channels to the public first.
